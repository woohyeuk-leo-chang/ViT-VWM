## Q1. Trained RNNs on delayed-report and WM tasks (generic task-optimized networks)

### Takeaway
Task-optimized RNNs reliably learn to hold continuous variables over delays. The code they learn (persistent attractor vs. dynamic/sequential vs. activity-silent synaptic) depends on training choices such as noise, delay variability, regularization, connectivity constraints and STP. Most of these papers study one or a few items, so they say little directly about set-size limits. The networks have no intrinsic capacity limit beyond what noise, size, or training impose.

### Cited Findings
- Orhan & Ma (2017), "Efficient probabilistic inference in generic neural networks trained with non-probabilistic feedback," *Nature Communications* 8:138. Generic networks trained only on error feedback, including tasks involving cue combination and integration over time, came to approximate near-optimal probabilistic inference. Sparse, heterogeneous codes emerged without being built in. This links trained networks to the "variable precision / uncertainty-aware" accounts of VWM. (K) — [DOI 10.1038/s41467-017-00181-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-017-00181-8)
- Orhan & Ma (2019), "A diverse range of factors affect the nature of neural representations underlying short-term memory," *Nature Neuroscience* 22:275–283. RNNs trained on delayed tasks fell anywhere between persistent attractor-like and sequential/dynamic memory codes. Which code appeared depended on task demands (e.g., delay variability), intrinsic circuit properties, and regularization/noise. So the "mechanism" an RNN finds is a product of training choices. This is a direct caution for interpreting a trained ViT. (K) — [DOI 10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y)
- Song, Yang & Wang (2016), "Training excitatory-inhibitory recurrent neural networks for cognitive tasks: a simple and flexible framework," *PLoS Computational Biology* 12:e1004792. E/I-constrained RNNs trained on WM tasks (e.g., parametric WM/vibrotactile frequency comparison) reproduce PFC-like mixed selectivity and dynamics. (K) — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004792](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004792)
- Yang, Joglekar, Song, Newsome & Wang (2019), "Task representations in neural networks trained to perform many cognitive tasks," *Nature Neuroscience* 22:297–306. One RNN trained on 20 tasks, including delayed match and memory-guided saccades, formed clusters of units specialized for task families. It also showed compositional task representations. (K) — [DOI 10.1038/s41593-018-0310-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0310-2)
- Masse, Yang, Song, Wang & Freedman (2019), "Circuit mechanisms for the maintenance and manipulation of information in working memory," *Nature Neuroscience* 22:1159–1167. RNNs with short-term synaptic plasticity (STSP) kept information "activity-silently" in synaptic efficacies for simple maintenance tasks. Persistent activity became necessary when information had to be manipulated. (K) — [DOI 10.1038/s41593-019-0414-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0414-3)
- Stroud, Watanabe, Suzuki, Stokes & Lengyel (2023/2024), "Optimal information loading into working memory explains dynamic coding in the prefrontal cortex," *PNAS* 120:e2307991120. For optimally loading inputs into attractor networks, inputs that first drive activity *orthogonal* to the persistent subspace are best. This explains the transient "dynamic coding" seen in PFC. (K; check year/volume) — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.2307991120](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2307991120)
- Xie, Hu, Zhou, Hu, Jiang, Wang, Liu, Liu, Xu, Xu, Wang & Lei? (2022), "Geometry of sequence working memory in macaque prefrontal cortex," *Science* 375:632–639. When monkeys held spatial sequences, PFC stored each ordinal rank in its own low-dimensional (ring-like) subspace. These subspaces were near-orthogonal, and their size shrank with rank. Trained RNNs reproduced the rank-specific subspaces. The geometry ties rank and order errors to the subspace structure. (K; author list after "Xie" not verified) — [DOI 10.1126/science.abm0204](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abm0204)
- Cueva, Saez, Marcos, Genovesio, Jazayeri, Romo, Salzman, Shadlen & Fusi (2020), "Low-dimensional dynamics for working memory and time encoding," *PNAS* 117:23021–23032. Trained RNNs and PFC both mix stable memory and time-varying components. (K) — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.1915984117](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1915984117). See also Cueva et al., arXiv 2111.01275, on RNN models of continuous-variable WM manifolds (V, title only) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.01275)
- Piwek, Stokes & Summerfield (2023), "A recurrent neural network model of prefrontal brain activity during a working memory task," *PLoS Computational Biology*. Surfaced in search, but the content was not verified. (V, title only) — [PLoS CB search hit via arXiv 2505.01178 refs](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01178)

### Inferences
- Orhan & Ma 2019 is the key warning for interpretability framing. The mechanism a trained network uses depends on training details, such as noise, regularization and the delay distribution. Mechanistic claims about a ViT should be tested for robustness across seeds and training regimes.
- Generic task-optimized RNNs usually show set-size effects only when noise or a bottleneck is added. Without these constraints, networks can memorize many items almost perfectly. Any capacity limit seen in a ViT trained on 1–8 items therefore needs a mechanistic explanation; it is not automatic.

### Gaps
- None of the classic RNN papers above (Song 2016, Yang 2019, Masse 2019) systematically varied set size and fit mixture models (guess rate, precision, swap rate) to network outputs. I found no canonical "trained RNN reproduces Zhang & Luck mixture parameters" paper.

## Q2. Mechanistic/circuit models that explicitly produce capacity limits, precision decline, and swap errors

### Takeaway
Mechanistic models produce limits in four main ways. In bump-attractor models, nearby bumps merge or a bump dies out (Wei et al. 2012). In normalized population codes, a fixed total spike budget is shared across items, which yields a resource-like precision decline (Bays 2014). Overlap in random/shared connectivity causes interference (Bouchacourt & Buschman 2019). Imprecise conjunctive or cue-feature binding in population codes produces swaps (Matthey et al. 2015; Schneegans & Bays 2017). Several of these models produce slot-like and resource-like signatures from the same continuous machinery.

### Cited Findings
- Bouchacourt & Buschman (2019), "A Flexible Model of Working Memory," *Neuron* 103(1):147–160. Structured sensory layers are coupled to a randomly connected, unstructured layer. Random recurrent connections keep arbitrary inputs active, so the network does not need content-tuned attractors. Overlap in the random connections makes representations interfere, which limits capacity: a flexibility–capacity tradeoff. The model reproduces several behavioral/neurophysiological WM signatures. (V) — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31103359/); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6613943/); [bioRxiv 407700](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/407700v1). Per the researcher's knowledge (K), the model's normalization/interference produces a soft capacity of about 3–4 items, precision that falls with load, and memories that drop out at high load.
- Bays (2014), "Noise in neural populations accounts for errors in working memory," *Journal of Neuroscience* 34:3632–3645. Poisson population coding with divisive normalization fixes the total activity shared across items. Fewer spikes per item means lower precision. Decoding gives heavy-tailed, apparently "guess-like" error distributions without any discrete slot limit. (K) — [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3204-13.2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3204-13.2014)
- Matthey, Bays & Dayan (2015), "A probabilistic palimpsest model of visual short-term memory," *PLoS Computational Biology* 11:e1004003. Items are stored by superimposing conjunctive (and feature) population codes in one layer, like a palimpsest. Sampling-based recall reproduces precision decline with set size. It also produces swap/misbinding errors that rise with load and with cue similarity. (K) — [DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004003](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004003)
- Schneegans & Bays (2017), "Neural architecture for feature binding in visual working memory," *Journal of Neuroscience* 37:3913–3925. In population models with conjunctive cue×feature coding, noise in the cue dimension causes swaps at retrieval. The models predict that swaps depend on cue-feature distance. (V via secondary mention; K details) — [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3493-16.2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3493-16.2017)
- Wei, Wang & Wang (2012), "From distributed resources to limited slots in multiple-item working memory: a spiking network model with normalization," *Journal of Neuroscience* 32:11228–11240. In a spiking ring attractor, lateral inhibition acts as normalization. At high load, nearby bumps merge and weak bumps vanish. The result is resource-like precision decline plus an emergent, slot-like item limit. (K) — [DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0735-12.2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0735-12.2012)
- Edin, Klingberg, Johansson, McNab, Tegnér & Compte (2009), "Mechanism for top-down control of working memory capacity," *PNAS* 106:6802–6807. In a bump-attractor model, lateral inhibition caps how many bumps can coexist. Top-down excitation from PFC to parietal cortex raises capacity. (K) — [DOI 10.1073/pnas.0901894106](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0901894106)
- Almeida, Barbosa & Compte (2015), "Neural circuit basis of visuo-spatial working memory precision: a computational and behavioral study," *J Neurophysiology* 114:1806–1818. Bump interactions cause attraction/repulsion biases between items. (K) — [DOI 10.1152/jn.00362.2015](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00362.2015)
- Nassar, Helmers & Frank (2018), "Chunking as a rational strategy for lossy data compression in visual working memory," *Psychological Review* 125:486–511. Chunking similar colors raises effective capacity at the cost of precision, a mechanism for between-item interference/attraction. (K) — [DOI 10.1037/rev0000101](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000101)
- Soni & Frank (2025), "Adaptive chunking improves effective working memory capacity in a prefrontal cortex and basal ganglia circuit," *eLife*. Reusing PFC populations for multiple items gives resource-like constraints inside a slot-like gating system. The model learns chunking. A meta-analysis found that chunking increases with set size in the best performers. (V) — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/97894); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40019141/)
- "Slot-like capacity and resource-like coding in a neural model of multiple-item working memory," *Journal of Neurophysiology* (2018; Standage & Paré). A further circuit model with both signatures. (V title; authors K) — [J Neurophysiol](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/full/10.1152/jn.00778.2017)
- 2025 *Scientific Reports*: a recurrent on-center/off-surround network with distance-dependent inhibition, modeling VWM capacity and precision (recent; details not fetched). (V title) — [Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-06919-5)
- Alleman, Panichello, Buschman & Johnston (2024), "The neural basis of swap errors in working memory," *PNAS*. Neural recordings show swap errors arise from misbinding/selection at report rather than random guessing. Author list (K). (V title) — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2401032121)
- Radmard, Bays & Lengyel (2025), arXiv 2505.01178. A Bayesian non-parametric mixture model of swap errors shows swaps depend on several factors (cue and feature distances). The authors pitch it explicitly as a source of training targets for network models. (V) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01178)
- Lindsay (2021), "Convolutional neural networks as a model of the visual system: past, present, and future," *Journal of Cognitive Neuroscience* 33:2017–2031. A review of how CNNs serve as models of the visual system and how they can be tested against brain data, including the limits of that approach. (K) — [DOI 10.1162/jocn_a_01544](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_01544)

### Inferences
- The circuit literature gives a ready list of mechanisms to look for in a ViT. Shared resources/normalization could correspond to softmax attention as a fixed-sum budget. Interference from overlapping codes could correspond to superposition in the residual stream. Binding noise in the cue dimension could correspond to positional-embedding imprecision causing swaps. Each maps onto a different swap and precision signature.
- The Bays 2014 and Wei 2012 results warn against reading a guess-rate component in a mixture fit as evidence of discrete slots. Continuous noise can produce the same pattern.

### Gaps
- I did not re-verify volume and page numbers for most canonical items in this session (marked K).

## Q3. Deep CNN/ANN image-computable models of VWM and efficient-coding accounts

### Takeaway
A 2022–2024 wave of image-computable models puts a pretrained deep visual encoder in front of a memory or decision stage. The strongest claim is Xie et al. (2023): human-like capacity limits arise when the sensory CNN is pretrained on natural images, not when the whole model is trained end-to-end on the WM task. Bates-style deep-feature/TCC models reproduce set-size effects but fit artificial color/orientation displays worse than natural scenes. In parallel, rate-distortion and efficient-coding theories treat capacity limits as optimal lossy compression under an information budget.

### Cited Findings
- Xie, Duan, Cheng, Jiang, Cueva & Yang (2023), "Natural constraints explain working memory capacity limitations in sensory-cognitive models," bioRxiv 10.1101/2023.03.30.534982. Raw image goes to a CNN "sensory" module, which feeds an RNN memory module. Tasks include change detection at set sizes such as 3, 5 and 10. Pretraining the sensory CNN on natural images was enough to produce a range of human-like VWM capacity behaviors, with no parameters fit to behavior. Analyses point to a bottom-up origin of the limit. A CCN 2022 abstract reports that limits appear with ImageNet-pretrained sensory systems *but not* with end-to-end WM training. Models did *not* reproduce the abrupt human drop at 3–4 items, and confidence tracked error spread at fixed set size. A talk version was titled "Sensory representation mismatch explains working memory capacity limitation." No journal version was found. (V) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.30.534982v1); [CCN 2022](https://2022.ccneuro.org/view_papercfd7.html?PaperNum=1251); [MIT BCS talk](https://bcs.mit.edu/events/cog-lunch-yudi-xie-sensory-representation-mismatch-explains-working-memory-capacity)
- "Scaling models of visual working memory to natural images," *Communications Psychology* (2024; article s44271-023-00048-3). Authors are, to the researcher's knowledge, Christopher J. Bates, George A. Alvarez & Samuel J. Gershman (K; not verified). The model combines DNN features with the Target Confusability Competition (TCC) model. Layers from DNN architectures reproduce set-size effects and response-bias curves for orientation and color. On GAN-generated naturalistic scenes, modern encoders explain coarse and some fine-grained trial-by-trial difficulty, which baselines do not. On artificial displays, though, deep encoders explained human data *worse* overall, despite reproducing set-size effects. Classification, CLIP and pixel-reconstruction objectives were judged unlikely to yield human-like memory errors. For the ViT-based architecture, the layer that best matched human data did not capture human-like errors. (V) — [Communications Psychology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-023-00048-3)
- Brady & Störmer (2023; authors K), "Comparing memory capacity across stimuli requires maximally dissimilar foils: Using deep convolutional neural networks to understand VWM capacity for real-world objects," *Memory & Cognition*. Target–foil dissimilarity, measured in DCNN feature space, is a primary determinant of performance. Capacity comparisons across stimulus classes are only fair with maximally dissimilar foils. (V) — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-023-01485-5)
- Schurgin, Wixted & Brady (2020), "Psychophysical scaling reveals a unified theory of visual memory strength," *Nature Human Behaviour* 4:1156–1172 (TCC model). Once psychological similarity is measured, a single memory-strength parameter (d′) explains continuous-report error distributions. Long tails are a product of similarity, not evidence of guessing. (K) — [DOI 10.1038/s41562-020-00938-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-00938-0)
- Hedayati, O'Donnell & Wyble (2022), "A model of working memory for latent representations," *Nature Human Behaviour* 6:709–719. A VAE (shape and color maps) feeds a shared "binding pool" that stores tokenized items. Because items share the pool, interference grows with load. Familiar items are stored compactly from high-level latents. Novel items are stored from early-layer latents. One item can have several codes, both detailed and categorical. The delay period is not modeled dynamically. (V) — [Nature Hum Behav](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01264-9); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.02.07.430171v4)
- Bates & Jacobs (2020), "Efficient data compression in perception and perceptual memory," *Psychological Review* 127:891–917. Rate-distortion theory and β-VAE-style models of perceptual memory reproduce set-size effects, category/prior biases and effects of task priority as optimal lossy compression. (K) — [DOI 10.1037/rev0000197](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000197)
- Sims (2015), "The cost of misremembering: Inferring the loss function in visual working memory," *Journal of Vision* 15(3):2. Also Sims, Jacobs & Knill (2012), *Psychological Review* 119:807–830. VWM is a capacity-limited information channel, and set-size effects follow from rate-distortion optimality. (K) — [DOI 10.1167/15.3.2](https://doi.org/10.1167/15.3.2); [DOI 10.1037/a0029856](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029856)
- Jakob & Gershman (2023), "Rate-distortion theory of neural coding and its implications for working memory," *eLife* 12:e79450. A population-coding circuit (inputs encode negative distortion, with global feedback inhibition) puts rate-distortion optimization into practice. It reproduces VWM regularities that earlier population models missed. Reviewers called the empirical fit qualitative. (V) — [eLife](https://elifesciences.org/articles/79450); [code](https://github.com/amvjakob/wm-rate-distortion)
- van den Berg & Ma (2018), "A resource-rational theory of set size effects in human visual working memory," *eLife* 7:e34963. Set-size effects come from a near-optimal tradeoff between performance and neural encoding cost. Sometimes it is optimal to encode only some items, or none. (V via bioRxiv) — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/151365v4.full); [DOI 10.7554/eLife.34963](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.34963)

### Inferences
- Xie et al. 2023 matters most for a pretrained ViT-B/16 project. It predicts that capacity limits may come from the pretrained representation (how ImageNet features encode multi-item displays and color), not from the readout or memory stage. That suggests a control experiment: compare a frozen vs. fine-tuned vs. from-scratch ViT, as Xie did with pretrained vs. end-to-end models.
- The Communications Psychology result predicts a specific failure mode. Pretrained encoders, including ViTs, may reproduce set-size effects on simple colored-square displays but miss fine-grained human error structure such as similarity-driven tails and biases.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the full Communications Psychology article (paywall redirect), so the author list and exact architectures (which CNN/ViT, which layers) are not verified.
- I could not confirm whether Xie et al. 2023 has a peer-reviewed journal version.

## Q4. Transformers/ViTs/LLMs on working-memory and VWM psychophysics tasks

### Takeaway
Direct tests of ViTs on continuous-report VWM with set-size manipulations are very sparse. I found no published paper that trains a ViT on a 1–8 item continuous-report color task and fits mixture/swap models, which suggests the downstream project fills a real gap. The nearest work covers small Transformers on N-back (an attention-entropy capacity limit), LLM working-memory benchmarks (failures of internal state), ViT perceptual biases, and VLM visual-cognition deficits.

### Cited Findings
- Gong & Zhang (2024), "Self-Attention Limits Working Memory Capacity of Transformer-Based Models," arXiv 2409.10715 / NeurIPS 2024 workshop. Small decoder-only Transformers trained on N-back show slight accuracy declines as N grows. Attention concentrates on the N-back position over training. Total attention entropy rises with N and correlates negatively with accuracy. The authors propose attention dispersion as the cause of the capacity limit. Models were tiny (e.g., one layer, one head). (V) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10715); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=dXjQgm9kAr)
- Gong, Wan & Wang (2024), "Working Memory Capacity of ChatGPT: An Empirical Study," AAAI 2024. ChatGPT's N-back performance declines with N in a human-like way. (K; authors not verified) — [arXiv 2305.03731](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03731)
- "Language Models Do Not Have Human-Like Working Memory" (2025), arXiv 2505.10571. LLMs fail to keep information internally without writing it into context, and CoT gives only marginal gains. Most LLM "WM" studies really measure context use. (V) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10571v3)
- "Exploring Working Memory Capacity in LLMs," IJCNLP 2025 (recent; not fetched). (V title) — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.93.pdf)
- Schulze Buschoff, Akata, Bethge & Schulz (2025), "Visual cognition in multimodal large language models," *Nature Machine Intelligence*. VLMs process visual data well but fall short of humans on core visual-cognition domains. (V; authors K) — [Nat Mach Intell](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00963-y)
- Tangtartharakul & Storrs (2026), *Nature Machine Intelligence*. VLMs show widespread low- and mid-level visual deficits on standardized neuropsychological tests, despite strong object recognition. **Most recent.** (V) — [Nat Mach Intell](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-026-01179-y)
- MaRs-VQA / "What is the Visual Cognition Gap between Humans and Multimodal LLMs?" (OpenReview 2025). A matrix-reasoning benchmark. It notes that multi-image reasoning and VWM in MLLMs are not well established. (V) — [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=78lTuD6wiO)
- "Vision Transformers Exhibit Human-Like Biases: Evidence of Orientation and Color Selectivity, Categorical Perception, and Phase Transitions" (2025), arXiv 2504.09393. ViTs show an oblique effect and color categorical perception, which bears on the color non-uniformities a ViT will bring to a hue-report task. (V) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09393)
- "Beyond Color Geometry: Evaluating Human-Like Color Representations in Vision Models" (2026 preprint, arXiv 2607.13647). Eleven ViT encoders were scored against a fuzzy human color-naming model, and MAE models scored highest. **Recent.** (V via aggregator) — [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2607.13647)
- Lepori, Tartaglini, Vong, Serre, Lake & Pavlick (2024), "Vision Transformers Represent Relations Between Objects," NeurIPS 2024. Mechanistic interpretability shows ViTs carry out same/different judgments in two stages: perceptual (object features) and then relational. The methods carry over to probing cue–item binding. (V; authors K) — [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/ed99503856b2ba85092e5413add65d86-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- Locatello et al. (2020), "Object-Centric Learning with Slot Attention," NeurIPS 2020. Slots compete through softmax attention and work like object files that bind any object. This is a natural architectural "slot" analogue, but I found no paper testing slot-attention models against human VWM set-size data. (V) — [NeurIPS](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/8511df98c02ab60aea1b2356c013bc0f-Paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- The softmax in self-attention normalizes to a fixed sum, so it resembles a shared resource directly (cf. Bays 2014 divisive normalization). Gong & Zhang's attention-entropy result is a Transformer version of "resource spreading." In a cued-report ViT, a natural hypothesis is that attention from the [CLS]/readout to the cued location's patch tokens becomes more diffuse as set size grows. More attention leaking to non-target items would produce swaps.
- Positional-embedding precision is a candidate for cue-dimension noise in the Schneegans & Bays sense. That predicts swaps should rise for items spatially near the cued item.

### Gaps
- I found no paper (2023–2026) that trains or fine-tunes a ViT on continuous-report VWM with set-size manipulation, or fits mixture/swap models to ViT outputs. This absence is informative but not proof: the search was limited to about 15 queries.
- I found no study that tests slot-attention/object-centric models against human set-size or swap data.

## Q5. Synthesis: which mechanisms were identified as the source of the limit?

### Takeaway
The literature names six main sources. (1) Neural noise plus a fixed activity budget or normalization gives resource-like precision decline (Bays 2014; Wei et al. 2012; Jakob & Gershman 2023). (2) Interference from overlapping or shared representations (Bouchacourt & Buschman 2019; Hedayati et al. 2022; Matthey et al. 2015). (3) Attractor dynamics, through bump merging/collapse and inhibition-limited bump counts, give slot-like item limits (Wei et al. 2012; Edin et al. 2009). (4) Binding noise in the cue dimension gives swaps (Matthey et al. 2015; Schneegans & Bays 2017; Alleman et al. 2024). (5) Sensory representations pretrained on natural images, a bottom-up limit (Xie et al. 2023). (6) Normative compression or resource-rational cost tradeoffs (Sims 2015; Bates & Jacobs 2020; van den Berg & Ma 2018). For Transformers specifically, attention dispersion has been proposed (Gong & Zhang 2024).

### Cited Findings
- Normalization/shared spikes → precision decline and apparent guesses — [Bays 2014](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3204-13.2014) (K)
- Random-connectivity interference → capacity–flexibility tradeoff — [Bouchacourt & Buschman 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31103359/) (V)
- Bump merging/extinction → slot-like limit from a resource model — [Wei, Wang & Wang 2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0735-12.2012) (K)
- Pretrained sensory front end → bottom-up limit; end-to-end training gives no human-like limit — [Xie et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.30.534982v1) (V)
- Training choices (noise, regularization, delay statistics) decide the RNN memory mechanism — [Orhan & Ma 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y) (K)
- Attention-score entropy rises with load in Transformers — [Gong & Zhang 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10715) (V)
- Rate-distortion optimal compression → set-size effects — [Jakob & Gershman 2023](https://elifesciences.org/articles/79450) (V); [Bates & Jacobs 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/rev0000197) (K)

### Inferences
- For the ViT-B/16 project, the most decisive comparisons follow from this literature. (a) Pretrained vs. scratch-trained ViT (Xie 2023). (b) Attention entropy/dispersion toward the cued item as a function of set size (Gong & Zhang; normalization accounts). (c) Swap rate as a function of spatial distance from the cue (Schneegans & Bays). (d) Mixture-model and TCC fits of model error distributions, since guess-like tails need not mean slots (Bays 2014; Schurgin et al. 2020). (e) Robustness of the identified mechanism across seeds and training regimes (Orhan & Ma 2019).
- A fine-tuned ViT with no noise and unlimited training may show *no* human-like limit, as end-to-end models in Xie 2023 did not. Any limit that does appear should be attributed carefully: does it come from the pretrained features, the 16×16 patching and position code, or the softmax budget?

### Gaps
- No quantitative head-to-head comparison of these mechanisms across models was found.
- The exact effect sizes (e.g., K estimates and precision slopes from Xie 2023 or Bates et al.) were not extracted, because the full texts could not be fetched (rate-limit/paywall).
