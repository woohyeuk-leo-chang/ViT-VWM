# Deep networks fail at selection, not storage

Current deep networks show some of the behavioral signatures of human visual working memory (VWM), but not for the reasons humans do. The pattern across ViTs, CNNs, RNNs, LLMs and VLMs is consistent. **Storage is rarely the bottleneck.** A transformer's tokens or KV cache hold every item losslessly, and task-trained RNNs show no intrinsic item limit unless noise or a bottleneck is imposed. **Selection, binding and retrieval under interference are where models break down, and in human-like ways.** VLMs show subitizing-like counting cliffs and illusory-conjunction errors, LLMs show load-dependent n-back declines and log-linear proactive interference, and small transformers spread their attention more as load grows. Image-computable models built on pretrained encoders reproduce set-size effects only partially: they miss the abrupt human drop at 3–4 items and fit simple colored-square displays worse than natural scenes. For ViT-VWM this is good news if the claim is framed carefully. The model sees memory and probe at the same time, so it cannot speak to maintenance. It can be the first mechanistic account of *where* a set-size limit arises inside a ViT, separating cue-blind encoding from cue-guided readout. The searches behind this report found no published study that fits mixture or swap models to a ViT on continuous report, or that measures how a ViT's target attention mass scales with the number of task-relevant items. The best near-term deliverable is a set of attention-budget, probing, swap-mechanism and activation-patching experiments run on the existing model once the mixture-fit bug is fixed and the control model is trained.

## Human VWM supplies four signatures, and none of them is theory-free

The yardstick is the delayed-estimation (continuous-report) task the project already copies. Its four core signatures are a set-size effect on precision, a heavy-tailed error distribution, swap errors toward non-target items, and stimulus-specific biases. Each has competing explanations, so a model cannot "match human VWM" without saying which reading of these signatures it is matching.

**Slot models** read the heavy tail as guessing. Zhang and Luck fit a von Mises-plus-uniform mixture to color-wheel reports and concluded that observers hold sharp representations of a few items and nothing about the rest ([Zhang & Luck 2008](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature06860)). On this view the probability in memory falls roughly as K/N once set size N exceeds a capacity K of about 3, and SD rises to K and then **plateaus**. Their stimulus space is a CIELAB circle at L* = 70 with radius 60 and set sizes 1, 2, 3 and 6 **[unverified number]**. This is close enough to the ViT-VWM wheel that the project can match it almost exactly. **Resource models** read the same data as a continuous quantity spread over all items. Precision falls as a power law with no plateau, even from 1 to 2 items ([Bays, Catalao & Husain 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19810788/)). Variable-precision models, which let precision vary across items and trials, account for "all aspects of the data, including apparent guessing" ([van den Berg et al. 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3365149/)).

The mechanistic version of the resource view is the most useful one for this project. In Bays's population-coding model, **divisive normalization holds total activity fixed across memoranda**, so per-item gain scales as 1/N. Decoding that noisy population produces heavy-tailed, guess-like errors without any slots, and error variance grows with load as a power law with **exponent 1.36** ([Bays 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3942580/)). A softmax over memory tokens is a fixed-sum budget of exactly this kind, which makes Bays 2014 the most natural bridge between human theory and the ViT-VWM readout.

Swap errors are the third signature and the one that matters most for interpretability. In cued continuous report a large share of big errors are reports of a **non-target's color**, caused by imprecise memory for the cue feature (here, location). The three-component model (target von Mises, non-target von Mises, uniform) is now the standard analysis ([Bays, Catalao & Husain 2009](https://www.paulbays.com/pdf/BayCatHus09.pdf)). Population models with conjunctive location×feature coding predict that swaps rise for non-targets whose location is near the cued location ([Schneegans & Bays 2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3493-16.2017)) **[unverified]**. Neural recordings place swaps at misbinding or selection during report, not random guessing ([Alleman et al. 2024](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2401032121)). The interference model goes further. It places the capacity limit in **competition among item–context bindings at retrieval**, with one item held in a protected focus of attention ([Oberauer & Lin 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27869455/)). That is the account closest to what a cue-driven cross-attention readout can produce.

Two cautions shape everything below. First, Target Confusability Competition (TCC) shows that measured psychological similarity on the color wheel falls off nonlinearly. With that similarity function, a single memory-strength parameter d′ fits whole error distributions, and the long tails do not imply guessing ([Schurgin, Wixted & Brady 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-00938-0)). Mixture "guess rates" and "precision" are therefore model-dependent quantities. One review argues they are not separable psychological constructs at all ([Brady et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02179-w)) **[unverified]**, and swap estimates are likewise not theory-free ([PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10270377/)). Second, **set-size effects exist for visible displays as well as remembered ones**, though they are smaller for visible displays ([Palmer 1990](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.16.2.332)) **[unverified]**. The dynamic neural resource model treats iconic memory and VWM as two regimes of one normalized population code, and it predicts a set-size effect even at zero delay ([Tomić & Bays 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/91034)). This is the closest human anchor for a no-delay network. For reference, typical human color-wheel SD is about 13–20° at N = 1, rising to about 20–25° for remembered items at N ≥ 3, with mixture K ≈ 3 **[unverified number]**. ViT-VWM's ≈5° mean error at N = 1 is already more precise than humans. The scientific interest lies in the *shape* of its decline, not its absolute level.

## Task-trained networks show limits only when something forces them

The recurrent-network literature is clear: networks trained on delayed tasks reliably learn to hold continuous variables, but **which mechanism they find depends on training choices.** Orhan and Ma showed that RNNs land anywhere between persistent attractor codes and sequential dynamic codes, depending on delay variability, regularization and noise ([Orhan & Ma 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y)) **[unverified]**. RNNs with short-term synaptic plasticity store information "activity-silently" for simple maintenance and switch to persistent activity when information must be manipulated ([Masse et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-019-0414-3)) **[unverified]**. None of the classic task-trained RNN papers varied set size systematically or fit mixture models to network outputs. Without noise or a bottleneck, these networks have no intrinsic capacity limit. The interpretability lesson is direct: any mechanism found in ViT-VWM must be shown to hold across seeds and training regimes before it counts as *the* mechanism.

Circuit models, by contrast, were built to produce limits, and they give a menu of mechanisms to look for. Normalized population codes give resource-like precision loss with guess-like tails ([Bays 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3942580/)). In spiking ring attractors, nearby bumps merge and weak bumps die at high load, so a resource-like mechanism produces an emergent slot-like limit ([Wei, Wang & Wang 2012](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.0735-12.2012)) **[unverified]**. Overlap in random recurrent connectivity produces interference and a flexibility–capacity tradeoff ([Bouchacourt & Buschman 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31103359/)). Superimposed conjunctive codes produce swaps that rise with load and cue similarity ([Matthey, Bays & Dayan 2015](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004003)) **[unverified]**. A shared VAE-fed "binding pool" produces interference that grows with the number of stored tokens ([Hedayati, O'Donnell & Wyble 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-021-01264-9)). Each of these maps onto a candidate ViT mechanism: softmax as fixed budget, residual-stream superposition as overlap, and positional-embedding imprecision as cue-dimension noise.

The image-computable models matter most for a pretrained ViT. Xie and colleagues passed raw images through a CNN "sensory" module into an RNN memory module. **Human-like capacity behavior appeared when the CNN was pretrained on natural images, not when the whole model was trained end-to-end on the memory task**, which points to a bottom-up origin of the limit. Even so, their models did not reproduce the abrupt human drop at 3–4 items ([Xie et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.30.534982v1); [CCN 2022 abstract](https://2022.ccneuro.org/view_papercfd7.html?PaperNum=1251)). Searches turned up no peer-reviewed journal version. A complementary result comes from visual search: pretrained CNNs with frozen features show set-size effects on simplified search displays, but networks trained from random initialization do not ([Nicholson & Prinz 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35675057)). The closest precedent to ViT-VWM combines DNN features with TCC. Intermediate layers reproduced set-size effects and bias curves for color and orientation, but deep encoders explained human data on *artificial* displays worse than on naturalistic scenes. Within CLIP-trained models, **the ViT fit humans worse than several CNNs** ([Communications Psychology 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-023-00048-3); [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.17.533050v2.full)). The authors are probably Bates, Alvarez and Gershman **[unverified]**. Crucially, in that model the set-size limit comes from the TCC noise model bolted on top, not from the network.

## Vision transformers and VLMs fail at binding many objects in one pass

The strongest behavioral evidence for human-like capacity limits in vision models comes from the binding-problem literature. Campbell and colleagues tested GPT-4v/4o, Gemini Ultra 1.5, Claude 3.5 Sonnet and text-to-image models. **Pop-out search did not depend on set size, while conjunction search degraded as objects were added**, starting from about 90% at 5 objects. Counting was accurate for 1–5 objects and **dropped sharply from 6**, roughly matching the human subitizing range. Scene-description errors scaled with the number of feature configurations prone to illusory conjunctions. Greater feature variability across objects *improved* performance ([Campbell et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00238); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00238v2)). The authors attribute this to shared compositional representations that interfere when several entities must be represented in one feedforward pass. Their account is behavioral; they did not open up the vision encoder.

Other benchmarks show the same cliff, and probing studies repeatedly find that **the information survives in the encoder even when the answer is wrong.** On BlindTest, four VLMs averaged 58.57% against a 24% chance baseline. Every model counted five overlapping Olympic-style circles perfectly and fell to near zero at six. A later version of the paper reports that linear probes on the vision encoders contain enough information to solve the tasks ([Rahmanzadehgervi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581)). A 2026 study recovered the correct count from hidden activations of four VLMs when the text answer was wrong, and fixed some failures with causal steering ([The Count Is There, but Misaligned](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09544v1)). Another attributes counting failures to diffuse attention that blurs object representations ([Can VLMs Count?](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17722v1)). For ViT-VWM this "stored but not read out" pattern is a testable prediction, not an assumption.

Mechanistic work now locates binding inside vision models. Pretrained ViTs encode a pairwise **IsSameObject** relation, decodable with quadratic probes at about 88–90% (against a 72.6% baseline) from mid-layers onward, in a low-dimensional subspace that guides attention ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24709); [code](https://github.com/liyihao0302/vit-object-binding)). A single-author 2026 preprint finds that this binding signal **decays exponentially with patch distance** toward a nonzero floor, a finite spatial horizon ([Singal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00006)). In VLMs, binding runs on a content-independent spatial indexing scheme, and binding errors trace to failures of those indices ([Assouel et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15871)). The vision encoder is the dominant source of spatial binding information, and amplifying vision-derived ordering directions fixed 40–55% of spatial failures. That study used only three objects and did not vary set size ([Cui et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22278)). Mean binding-difference vectors added to object tokens predictably swap associations in VLMs ([Saravanan, Tapaswi & Gandhi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22200)). CLIP-style encoders handle single objects well but break when attributes must be bound to the right object ([Lewis et al. 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-eacl.101/)).

There is also a formal reason to expect set-size effects in a softmax readout. **Any learned softmax circuit must disperse as the number of competing items grows** at test time ([Veličković et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01104)) **[unverified]**. Single-head selection drifts toward uniform as more tokens must be distinguished ([Mudarisov et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17821)). In small transformers trained on n-back, **total attention entropy rises with n and correlates negatively with accuracy** ([Gong & Zhang 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10715)). One practical confound is that ViTs repurpose low-information background patches as high-norm tokens that absorb attention and store global information ([Darcet et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16588)). ViT-VWM's memory image is mostly blank background, which is exactly that regime.

## Language models store perfectly but retrieve under interference

The LLM literature supplies the cleanest version of the storage-versus-retrieval distinction. On in-context n-back, ChatGPT's d′ fell with n, giving a capacity of "around 3" verbally and less spatially. The authors call this "strikingly similar to humans" ([Gong, Wan & Wang 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03731)). The stimuli never leave the context window, though, so this cannot be a storage limit. The proactive-interference paradigm makes the point sharply. Models see a stream of key–value updates and must report each key's latest value. **Accuracy falls log-linearly toward zero as interference accumulates**, even though the answer sits just before the query and the input is far below the context limit. Llama-4 Maverick falls from about 100% with 2 tracked keys to under 5% with 46. Errors are mostly overwritten earlier values. Resistance scales with parameter count, not context length (t = 3.03, p = 0.005), and "forget" instructions barely help ([Wang & Sun 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.08184v3)).

The mechanism has now been opened up. Pretrained LLMs on a working-memory task show human-like load effects, recency bias and stimulus-statistics biases. **Items are encoded in entangled "in-context superposition," and successive layers suppress competitors and align the target with the readout.** Suppressing interfering information causally improves performance, and working-memory capacity correlates with general capability, as it does in humans ([Xiong et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09670)). A tiny two-layer transformer trained directly on the task solves it perfectly, which echoes Xie's end-to-end result: the limit comes from shared pretrained codes, not from the architecture alone. On the other side, LLMs **essentially lack latent maintenance.** Asked to privately "pick" a number and then answer yes/no about each value, most models' yes-probabilities summed to about 0 instead of 1, and chain-of-thought gains came only from externalizing state ([Huang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.10571v3)).

Binding is where LLM capacity limits live mechanistically. Entities and attributes carry matching binding-ID vectors, and **the distance between binding vectors tracks how discernible the bindings are** ([Feng & Steinhardt 2023/2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191)). Retrieval mixes positional, lexical and reflexive pointers. The positional signal is reliable at list edges and diffuse in the middle, which is proposed as the cause of lost-in-the-middle ([Gur-Arieh, Geva & Geiger 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06182)). Fine-tuning strengthens an existing entity-tracking circuit, mainly by improving positional information, rather than building a new one ([Prakash et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811)). Fixed-state architectures (SSMs, linear attention) do have hard recall limits that shrink with state size, which makes them the closest architectural analogue of a classic capacity-limited store ([Arora et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.04927); [Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)) **[unverified]**.

## The honest answer: retrieval limits yes, storage and maintenance limits mostly no

Putting the themes together gives a bottom line that is defensible in a lab meeting. Deep networks exhibit **some** human VWM characteristics. Where they do, the characteristics live in **selection, binding and retrieval under interference**, not in a limited store or in maintenance over time. The resemblance is behavioral and partial. Models reproduce graded set-size costs, interference-driven errors and (in VLMs) a cliff around 5–6 items, but no model class has convincingly reproduced the human combination of a K ≈ 3 inflection, location-proximity-dependent swaps and human-scaled similarity structure. The table summarizes the evidence by model class.

| Model class | Set-size / load effect | Swap / binding errors | Storage limited? | Latent maintenance? | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Task-trained RNNs | Only with noise or bottlenecks | Not systematically tested | Only if imposed | Yes (persistent or activity-silent) | Orhan & Ma 2019; Masse et al. 2019 [unverified] |
| Circuit models | Yes, by design (normalization, bump merging) | Yes (conjunctive codes) | Yes, by design | Yes | Bays 2014; Wei et al. 2012; Matthey et al. 2015 |
| Pretrained CNN + memory or TCC | Yes, but no abrupt 3–4 drop; set-size limit partly from the noise model | Not central | Partly: inherited from pretrained features | Not tested, or by design | Xie et al. 2023; Comm. Psych. 2024; Nicholson & Prinz 2022 |
| ViTs / VLMs | Yes: counting and conjunction-search cliffs | Yes: illusory conjunctions, binding-index failures | Often no: probes recover the answer | N/A (single pass) | Campbell et al. 2024; Rahmanzadehgervi et al. 2024; Cui et al. 2026 |
| LLMs | Yes: n-back about 3, log-linear interference | Yes: binding-ID crowding, overwritten values | No: lossless context | Largely absent | Gong et al. 2024; Wang & Sun 2025; Xiong et al. 2026; Huang et al. 2025 |

Two steelman objections deserve a hearing. The first is that resemblance is cheap: a heavy-tailed error distribution can come from normalization noise, similarity structure or genuine guessing, so matching a curve proves little ([Schurgin et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-00938-0); [Bays 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3942580/)). That is precisely why interpretability helps here. Internal measurements can tell those mechanisms apart where behavior cannot. The second objection is that human VWM limits may themselves be largely retrieval and interference limits ([Oberauer & Lin 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27869455/); [Alleman et al. 2024](https://www.pnas.org/doi/abs/10.1073/pnas.2401032121)). If so, "selection, not storage" would be a point of genuine convergence between models and humans, not a disanalogy. The evidence cannot yet settle this, and saying so is part of the honest answer.

## ViT-VWM fills an empty niche if it claims selection, not maintenance

### The gap is real but narrowly defined

Across roughly 15 targeted queries per theme, the research notes found **no published study, through October 2026, that trains or fine-tunes a ViT on continuous-report color memory with a set-size manipulation and fits mixture or swap models to its outputs.** They also found no study that measures how a ViT's target attention mass scales with the number of task-relevant items, and none that probes ViT patch tokens for color–location binding as a function of item count. The nearest neighbors either have the network without the paradigm (Campbell's VLM behavior, Li's IsSameObject probes), or the paradigm without a network-internal limit (DNN features plus TCC). This absence is informative, not proof. CogSci 2025–2026 proceedings and recent workshop papers were not fully indexed, so a targeted check before any public claim of novelty is warranted.

### The architecture already factorizes encoding from retrieval

Reading the code in `vit_vwm/models.py` sharpens the framing. The memory and probe images go through the encoder in **separate forward passes**, so memory tokens are computed **without knowledge of which item will be cued**. Blocks 0–10 are frozen, so memory-token representations up to block 10 are pure ImageNet-pretrained features, identical across every trained variant. The probe query is a max-pool over probe tokens passed through a small MLP adapter. Cross-attention is a **single head with no key or value projections**: the query dot-products directly with all 197 memory tokens (CLS and background included) at a fixed 1/√768 scale. The retrieved vector is therefore a literal convex combination of memory tokens, decoded by an MLP into [sin, cos]. This is close to an ideal interpretability testbed. Encoding is cue-blind, retrieval is one softmax, and the retrieved vector decomposes exactly into per-token contributions.

### The framing risk, and how to phrase the claim

Because memory and probe arrive together with no delay, mask or blank, ViT-VWM measures **encoding and selection capacity**, not maintenance. It has no delay over which to test decay, interference over time, or persistent versus activity-silent storage. The closest human analogue is a simultaneous or zero-delay cue condition, where set-size effects exist but are smaller ([Palmer 1990](https://doi.org/10.1037/0096-1523.16.2.332) **[unverified]**; [Tomić & Bays 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/91034)), or a Sperling-style partial report. Calling the model "a working memory model" invites the first reviewer question in any cognitive-science audience. The LLM and VLM literature turns the limitation into a selling point: the human-like part of transformer "working memory" is selection under interference, and this design isolates selection cleanly.

A defensible one-sentence claim is: *"We fine-tune a ViT on a cued continuous-report color task and use mechanistic interpretability to locate the source of its set-size limit, distinguishing cue-blind encoding from cue-guided selection. We evaluate the result against signatures from human visual working memory, without claiming the model implements maintenance."* Phrases to avoid are "the ViT has a working memory capacity of K items" and "the ViT forgets." A natural extension, if the project grows, is a sequential version (memory frame, blank or mask, then probe, carried through a CLS or register bottleneck), which would move the model from the unbounded-buffer regime into a fixed-state regime ([Jelassi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01032)) **[unverified]**.

### Fix the measurement layer before running the mechanistic experiments

Three fixes come first, because every experiment below depends on them. The mixture fits need float64 inputs. The errors are stored as float32, so the finite-difference gradients L-BFGS-B computes vanish and the optimizer stops at its initial guess; this was confirmed by re-running the fit on simulated data (float64 recovers κ≈20, float32 returns the starting values). Casting `error_rad` to float64 fixes it; using `scipy.special.i0e` or log-space densities additionally guards against I₀(κ) overflow at the very high κ expected at N = 1. The mean-pool control must be trained. It is the binding-blind lower bound: mean-pooling memory tokens discards most location information, so it should approach chance-like errors for N > 1 unless location leaks into pooled features. Results must also be reported per set size, with at least three seeds, because the mixed-set-size 34° mean conceals the curve's shape and the RNN literature shows that mechanisms vary with training details ([Orhan & Ma 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y) **[unverified]**).

## Five experiments that turn a set-size curve into a mechanism

The five experiments below run in priority order. The first two need only forward hooks on the existing model and one activation cache. They can produce lab-meeting figures within a week.

| # | Experiment | Core metric | Builds on | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Attention budget vs set size | Target attention mass, entropy, distractor mass; trial-level correlation with error | Gong & Zhang 2024; Bays 2014; Veličković et al. 2024; Darcet et al. 2024 | Hours |
| 2 | Storage vs readout dissociation | Circular-probe angular error by layer and N; "readout gap" | Rahmanzadehgervi et al. 2024; Count Is There 2026; Hewitt & Liang 2019; Xie et al. 2023 | 1–2 days |
| 3 | Swap vs averaging mechanism | 3-component mixture and TCC parameters; attention-weighted prediction fit; swap vs cue distance | Bays et al. 2009; Bays 2016; Schurgin et al. 2020; Singal 2026 | 2–3 days |
| 4 | Causal interchange interventions | Normalized angular shift toward source hue | Heimersheim & Nanda 2024; Geiger et al. 2021; Saravanan et al. 2025; Feng & Steinhardt | ~1 week |
| 5 | Origin of the limit (controls) | Set-size slope and mixture parameters across variants and seeds | Xie et al. 2023; Nicholson & Prinz 2022; Orhan & Ma 2019 | Stretch (training runs) |

### Experiment 1 tests whether softmax acts as a fixed resource

For every trial, use the stimulus generator's square masks to compute the cross-attention mass on patches overlapping the cued square (target mass), on each distractor square, on background patches, on CLS, and on high-norm tokens (flag tokens whose norm is far above the median, following [Darcet et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16588)). Also compute attention entropy H and the effective number of attended tokens, exp(H). The resource hypothesis predicts that target mass falls and entropy rises with N, as in [Gong & Zhang 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10715). A Bays-style account predicts that per-item mass scales near 1/N and that the log–log slope of error variance against N approaches the human exponent of about 1.36 ([Bays 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3942580/)). The decisive statistic is trial-level: does target mass predict absolute error *within* a set size? If target mass stays high while error rises, the limit lies elsewhere, in the encoded features or the MLP readout. That outcome is just as publishable. Separating out mass absorbed by high-norm background tokens is essential, because softmax mass must go somewhere ([Xiao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.17453)) **[unverified]**.

### Experiment 2 asks whether the hue is stored but not selected

Cache memory tokens at every layer and train low-capacity circular probes (ridge regression onto [sin, cos], scored in mean absolute angular error). Each probe decodes item k's hue from (a) the patch tokens at item k's location and (b) all tokens pooled. Run the probes separately per set size, and report Hewitt–Liang selectivity against a control task with shuffled but consistent hue labels ([Hewitt & Liang 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.03368) **[unverified]**; [Belinkov 2022](https://aclanthology.org/2022.cl-1.7/) **[unverified]**). Then decode target and non-target hues from the *retrieved* vector after attention. The key quantity is the **readout gap**: behavioral error minus best-probe error at each N. Flat probe error with rising behavioral error means a selection limit, which would match the VLM pattern ([Rahmanzadehgervi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581); [Count Is There 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09544v1)) and the LLM pattern ([Xiong et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09670)). Probe error that rises with N already in frozen layers 0–10 means an encoding limit inherited from ImageNet pretraining, which is Xie's bottom-up story ([Xie et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.30.534982v1)). Because blocks 0–10 are frozen, that layer range gives a clean answer about pretrained features. Probes show decodability, not use, so Experiment 4 supplies the causal test.

### Experiment 3 separates discrete swaps from attention-weighted averaging

Once the float64 fix is in, fit the two-component Zhang–Luck model and the three-component Bays model per set size, along with the nonparametric swap estimator ([Bays 2016](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep19203)). Run parameter recovery on simulated data, since swap and uniform components become hard to identify when eight non-target hues cover the wheel. Then use the architecture to run a test human studies cannot. Because the retrieved vector is a convex combination of memory tokens, compare two trial-level predictors of the model's response. An **averaging** predictor uses the circular mean of item hues weighted by each item's attention mass. A **swap** predictor uses the hue of the most-attended item. Averaging predicts responses biased toward the weighted mean, which can land on a hue no item has. Swaps predict modes at non-target hues. Next, regress swap probability on cue–distractor spatial distance (adjacent locations versus opposite ones on the 8-location circle). Population-coding accounts and the finite spatial horizon of ViT binding both predict more swaps for near neighbors ([Schneegans & Bays 2017](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3493-16.2017) **[unverified]**; [Singal 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00006)). Two further manipulations are cheap. Vary hue diversity at fixed N, since Campbell found that feature variability reduces interference ([Campbell et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00238)). And fit TCC using a **network-internal similarity function**, cosine similarity of single-square embeddings across hues, to test whether one d′ per set size explains the model's error shapes ([Schurgin et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-020-00938-0)). Check the network's color nonuniformities too, since ViTs show color categorical perception ([ViT biases 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09393)).

### Experiment 4 makes the binding claim causal

The stimulus generator makes minimal pairs trivial, which is the main advantage over natural-image interpretability. Build pairs that differ only in (a) the target hue, (b) one distractor's hue, (c) the positions of two swapped items, or (d) the cue location. Patch memory tokens (target patches, distractor patches, background, high-norm tokens) at the input and output of block 11. Separately, patch the probe query between cue locations. Score each patch by the **normalized angular shift** of the predicted hue toward the source hue, where 0 means no effect and 1 means full transfer. Follow [Heimersheim & Nanda 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15255) on separating exploratory sweeps from confirmatory tests. If patching only location-carrying components makes the model report a distractor's hue, that is direct evidence for a misbinding mechanism, the visual analogue of binding-ID swaps ([Feng & Steinhardt](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.17191); [Saravanan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22200)). A stretch goal is to learn the location subspace of the query with DAS ([Geiger et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.02536) **[unverified]**) and validate it against the subspace-patching illusion ([Makelov, Lange & Nanda 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17030) **[unverified]**). A cross-model patch between pretrained and fine-tuned block 11 asks whether fine-tuning remapped existing features or built new ones ([Prakash et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14811); [Lim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05276)).

### Experiment 5 locates the limit's origin with controlled variants

This is the most compute-heavy experiment, and the one a skeptical reviewer will want. Compare the set-size slope and mixture parameters across five variants: the mean-pool control (no selection); the current model; more trainable blocks (the last four, then full fine-tuning); a randomly initialized ViT-B/16 trained from scratch; and a sharper readout, using a learned temperature or two heads. Xie's and Nicholson and Prinz's results predict that the from-scratch model will show a weaker human-like set-size effect ([Xie et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2023.03.30.534982v1); [Nicholson & Prinz 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35675057)). Softmax-dispersion theory predicts that adaptive temperature flattens the slope if the attention budget is causal ([Veličković et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01104) **[unverified]**). Run at least three seeds per variant. Mechanisms that appear in only one seed should be reported as such ([Orhan & Ma 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-018-0314-y) **[unverified]**).

### Tooling

PyTorch forward hooks on the timm model, or nnsight for patching inside the custom head ([nnsight](https://github.com/ndif-team/nnsight)) **[unverified]**, are simpler than forcing the custom cross-attention head into ViT-Prisma ([Prisma](https://github.com/Prisma-Multimodal/ViT-Prisma)). Cache 197×768 memory tokens per layer, plus the query and attention weights, once for a balanced set of set sizes, hues and locations. Every probe, attention and patching analysis can then read from that cache.

## Lab meeting talking points

1. Bottom line: deep networks show human-like limits in *selecting and binding* items, not in *storing* them. LLMs and VLMs hold everything but retrieve under interference, and the answer is often decodable when the output is wrong.
2. ViT-VWM cannot claim working-memory maintenance, because there is no delay. It can claim to locate where a set-size limit arises in a ViT, separating cue-blind encoding (frozen pretrained features) from cue-guided selection (one softmax readout).
3. The gap: no published study found fits mixture or swap models to a ViT on continuous report, or measures target attention mass against item count. A final novelty check of CogSci and workshop papers is still pending.
4. Current status: ≈5° at N = 1 (better than humans) and ≈34° averaged over mixed set sizes. The mixture fits have a confirmed float32 precision bug (the optimizer never leaves its initial guess), and the mean-pool control is untrained. Both are fixable this week.
5. First two experiments: attention budget against N (does target mass fall like 1/N, and does it predict error within a set size?), and a storage-versus-readout probe (is the hue still decodable when the model gets it wrong?).
6. Distinctive test: the retrieved vector is literally an attention-weighted average of tokens, so we can tell attention-weighted *averaging* from discrete *swaps*. Human data cannot make that distinction directly.
7. Ask for the lab: which human comparison dataset to use (the OSF benchmark sets from [Oberauer et al. 2018](https://osf.io/g49c6/)), and whether to add a delayed or sequential variant later.

## Conclusion

The "do deep networks have working memory?" question dissolves once storage, maintenance and selection are separated. Transformers have an essentially unlimited store, almost no latent maintenance, and a selection stage that fails in graded, interference-driven ways resembling the interference and normalization accounts of human VWM. That reframes ViT-VWM's apparent weakness, simultaneous presentation, as a design that isolates the one component where model–human comparison is both meaningful and mechanistically tractable. The architecture's single-query, projection-free readout makes the hardest question in human VWM research (is the tail guessing, swapping or averaging?) directly measurable.

The most valuable outcome is not a match to human curves but a dissociation. One possibility is that hue information survives in frozen pretrained tokens while the softmax readout fails as N grows. The other is that ImageNet features already blur multi-item displays before any task-specific computation. Either result is a crisp, falsifiable interpretability finding that links to Bays's normalization, Xie's bottom-up origin, and Xiong's suppress-then-read-out circuits. It also translates directly into the skill set interpretability internships look for: causal interventions, probing with controls, and honest claims about what a measurement can and cannot show.
